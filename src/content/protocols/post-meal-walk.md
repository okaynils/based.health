### What to do
Walk for 10 minutes after your largest meal. Add other meals later if it feels easy.

1. Start within 30 minutes after eating.
2. Walk at an easy pace.
3. Keep it boring and repeatable: outside, hallway, stairs, or errands.
4. If 10 minutes is too much, do 2-5 minutes.

### Why it works
Muscle contractions help move glucose out of the bloodstream. A short walk also breaks the sit-eat-sit loop that dominates many days.

### Modify or avoid
If walking after meals causes dizziness, chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, or hypoglycemia symptoms, stop and get medical guidance.

### Evidence
Trials and reviews show that interrupting sedentary time with light activity can improve post-meal glucose and insulin responses.[^1] Effects are especially relevant for people with impaired glucose control, but the protocol is low-friction for most adults.

[^1]: [Dempsey et al. (2016): Interrupting prolonged sitting and postprandial glucose, insulin, and triglycerides](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27510558/)
