### What to do
Get outside within 30-60 minutes of waking. Face the bright part of the sky, not the sun.

1. Go outside soon after waking.
2. Stay out for 5-10 minutes on bright days.
3. Use 15-30 minutes on cloudy or dark winter mornings.
4. Skip sunglasses for this short exposure if comfortable. Glasses or contacts are fine.
5. Repeat daily, especially after travel, late nights, or schedule drift.

### Why it works
Light is the strongest timing cue for the circadian system. Brighter morning light tends to shift sleep timing earlier, while bright evening light tends to push it later.[^1]

Outdoor light is usually much brighter than indoor light, even on cloudy days. The point is not tanning or staring at the sun. The point is giving the retina a strong "day has started" signal.

### Modify or avoid
Avoid direct sun-gazing. If you have retinal disease, severe light sensitivity, migraines triggered by light, or medication that increases photosensitivity, use clinician guidance and gentler exposure.

### Evidence
Human sleep timing changes with the timing and amount of light exposure. Reviews of community and clinical studies support morning bright light as a circadian phase-advancing tool, though exact dose varies by person, season, and latitude.[^1][^2]

[^2]: [Zee et al. (2008): Treatment of circadian rhythm sleep disorders with light](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18797560/)
