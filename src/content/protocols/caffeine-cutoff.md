### What to do
Set a caffeine cutoff 8 hours before bedtime. If sleep is still fragile, move it to 10-12 hours.

1. Decide your target bedtime.
2. Count back 8 hours.
3. Put coffee, energy drinks, pre-workout, and strong tea before that time.
4. Keep total caffeine below 400 mg/day unless a clinician told you otherwise.
5. Use decaf or non-caffeinated drinks after the cutoff.

### Why it works
Caffeine blocks adenosine signaling. That can feel useful during the day, but late caffeine can reduce sleep time and sleep quality even when you still fall asleep.

### Modify or avoid
Some people metabolize caffeine slowly. If you wake during the night, feel wired at bedtime, or need caffeine to feel normal, lower the dose and move the cutoff earlier.

### Evidence
A controlled sleep study found that 400 mg caffeine taken even 6 hours before bedtime disrupted sleep.[^1] The FDA cites 400 mg/day as an amount not generally associated with dangerous effects for most adults, but sensitivity varies.[^2]

[^1]: [Drake et al. (2013): Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before bed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24235903/)
[^2]: [FDA: Spilling the beans, how much caffeine is too much?](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/spilling-beans-how-much-caffeine-too-much)
