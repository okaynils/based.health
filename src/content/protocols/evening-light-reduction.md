### What to do
Make the final 90 minutes before bed dimmer and warmer.

1. Turn off overhead lights.
2. Use lamps, warm bulbs, or low brightness.
3. Put screens in night mode and reduce brightness.
4. Keep bright admin, training, and stressful work earlier.
5. Make the bedroom dark.

### Why it works
Light at night can delay circadian timing and suppress the body's night signal. You do not need a perfect cave. You need a reliable contrast between day and night.

### Modify or avoid
Do not make your environment unsafe. If you work nights, use a shift-work-specific protocol instead of copying a daytime schedule.

### Evidence
Light exposure timing affects sleep timing. A systematic review found evening light was associated with later sleep timing, while morning light tended to move timing earlier.[^1] Controlled studies also show light can suppress melatonin depending on intensity and timing.[^2]

[^1]: [Dautovich et al. (2019): Light amount and timing with sleep outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6814154/)
[^2]: [Gooley et al. (2011): Exposure to room light before bedtime suppresses melatonin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21193540/)
