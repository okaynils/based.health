### What to do
Find your current average steps, then add a small baseline.

1. Track normal steps for 7 days.
2. Set a daily floor 1,000 steps above that average.
3. Hold it for 2 weeks.
4. Add another 500-1,000 steps if recovery is fine.
5. Use short walks, errands, calls, or commute changes.

### Why it works
Steps are not magic. They are a simple proxy for low-intensity physical activity, which is easier to accumulate than formal exercise.

### Modify or avoid
If your joints or feet flare up, reduce the increase and spread steps through the day. Supportive shoes and softer surfaces can help.

### Evidence
Physical activity guidelines recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity weekly, and walking is one of the simplest ways to accumulate it.[^1] Step-count studies associate higher daily steps with lower mortality risk, with benefits appearing well below 10,000 steps for many groups.[^2]

[^1]: [CDC: Adult physical activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)
[^2]: [Paluch et al. (2022): Steps per day and all-cause mortality meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34417979/)
